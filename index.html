The read

Good news first: nothing has been lost. All 280 posts, 15 pages, your media library and your plugins are intact, and Jetpack has completed a successful full backup every single day through the outage. The domain is paid up until May 2028. This is a configuration problem, not a data loss, and it is fixable.

What appears to have happened. On the evening of November 4, the site's address switched from your custom domain to its WordPress.com staging address. The domain itself is still attached to the account, but its status is "connection error" and its DNS records no longer point at WordPress.com — so a visitor typing teamintegrityknowledgecenter.com, or clicking a Google result, does not reach your site. The site is running perfectly at an address that nobody knows.

The 1-to-2 views a day are almost certainly your own team. Whoever has the staging URL open in a tab. That is the only route into the site that still works, which is why the number is so precisely small and so consistent.

Search traffic fell 68% and will keep falling. Search went from roughly 59 to 19 views, and almost all of those 19 came on November 3 and 4 before the break. Google and Bing are now crawling URLs that do not respond. If this runs much longer, those pages start dropping out of the index — and getting re-indexed afterwards takes considerably longer than the outage itself. That is the part that compounds.

Five posts were published on November 5, into a site nobody could reach. Everything published since November 4 has had no audience at all. Anything scheduled this week will land the same way until the domain is fixed.

Your newsletter links are broken too. Every link in The Briefing and every Long Read send points at teamintegrityknowledgecenter.com. Any email that went out after November 4 drove subscribers to a page that does not load. Worth checking what has sent since, because that affects deliverability as well as traffic.

Roughly 400 views lost so far, and that is the small cost. At the site's recent baseline of about 32 views a day, twelve days of outage is somewhere near 400 views that did not happen. The larger cost is search ranking and the impression left on anyone who clicked a link and found nothing — including anyone who arrived from a newsletter, a Facebook post, or a business card.

The domain is also not set to auto-renew. It is paid through May 2028 so there is no urgency, but while someone is in the domain settings fixing this, turning auto-renew on costs nothing and removes a future version of this exact problem.

773 subscribers, ninth consecutive report. Unchanged again — though for once that is not the headline.

What to do next

The first item is the only one that matters this week.

  1. Confirm it yourself — ten secondsOpen teamintegrityknowledgecenter.com in a private browser window on your phone. If it does not load your site, this report is right and item two is the fix. Do this before anything else, because everything below depends on it.
  2. Point the domain's DNS back at WordPress.comThis is a registrar-level change and needs whoever controls the domain account — Hamza handles this kind of configuration. The root domain's A records need to be set to 192.0.78.171 and 192.0.78.242. Once DNS propagates, the SSL certificate issues automatically. Then set teamintegrityknowledgecenter.com back as the site's primary address in WordPress.com → Settings → Domains.
  3. Pause publishing and all newsletter sends until it is backFive posts went out on November 5 to nobody. Every send and every post between now and the fix is wasted effort, and newsletter links pointing at a dead domain hurt your sender reputation on top of it.
  4. Once it is live, resubmit the sitemap to Google and BingBoth have been crawling broken URLs for nearly two weeks. Resubmitting teamintegrityknowledgecenter.com/post-sitemap.xml in Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools tells them to re-crawl immediately rather than waiting for the next natural pass.
  5. Turn on Jetpack MonitorYour plan already includes downtime alerts. Had Monitor been active, you would have had an email within minutes of November 4 instead of finding out twelve days later from a traffic report. This takes one click.
  6. Turn on domain auto-renew while you are in therePaid through May 2028, so not urgent — but it is currently off, and a lapsed domain produces exactly this outage with a harder recovery.
